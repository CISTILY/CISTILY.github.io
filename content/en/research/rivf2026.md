## Research question

Can a parameter-efficient vision-language model improve classification of musculoskeletal radiographs by combining image details with clinical history, while also providing useful analyses of distribution shift and prediction dependence?

XBone-Net studies this question in two settings: CTCH, a private patient-disjoint dataset of 3,249 radiograph and clinical-history pairs across 22 classes, and BTXRD, a public 10-class bone-tumor dataset of 3,746 radiographs. CTCH uses clinical histories; BTXRD has no free-text histories, so its text is generated offline from dataset metadata. Results on these text sources are not directly interchangeable.

**The project addresses several practical challenges:**
- **Limited and imbalanced medical data:** Some classes have substantially fewer examples than others, making reliable classification difficult.
- **Fine detail in radiographs:** Resizing a full image to a fixed square can obscure small structures.
- **Image-only context:** A radiograph is interpreted alongside clinical history in practice, but many models do not use this information.
- **Resource constraints:** Full fine-tuning of a large vision-language model updates many parameters and requires substantial training memory.
- **Reliability under shift:** Classification scores alone do not show whether a sample differs from the training distribution or which modality influences a prediction.

We therefore develop and evaluate a two-stage framework built on BiomedCLIP, with parameter-efficient LoRA adaptation, multimodal fusion, post-hoc out-of-distribution (OOD) scoring, and Integrated Gradients (IG) analyses.

## Approach

We evaluate XBone-Net as a multimodal classification and reliability-auditing framework, comparing it with image-only configurations, established vision baselines, full fine-tuning, and component ablations.

### Data and input representation

Each case contains a radiograph and its associated text. For CTCH, the text is clinical history; for BTXRD, it is generated from metadata, including disease-group information. The image pipeline retains both global anatomy and local detail: it produces one global view and four deterministic, label-free local crops selected from image content. No lesion annotations are needed for crop selection.

The experiments use three random seeds (42, 123, and 456) on fixed data partitions. The CTCH data are patient-disjoint. The datasets and patient-level data are not distributed with the source code; reproducibility therefore requires access to the corresponding prepared data and split files.

### Parameter-efficient multimodal adaptation

XBone-Net uses BiomedCLIP image and text encoders, adapting their linear layers with low-rank adaptation (LoRA) while keeping pretrained backbone weights frozen. Training has two stages:

1. **Semantic alignment:** image and text representations are adapted to the target domain using a soft semantic matching objective.
2. **Fusion and classification:** bidirectional cross-attention combines image and text features, followed by a class-centroid classification head with class-balanced training.

The model updates approximately 8.44 million parameters, or 4.13% of its total parameters. This reduces peak GPU training memory by more than 60% relative to full BiomedCLIP fine-tuning in the reported experiments. Parameter efficiency does not make the complete system computationally cheaper: training takes about 2.0–2.5 times longer, and inference computation and memory are slightly higher.

### OOD and explanation analyses

The project evaluates post-hoc OOD warning scores derived from the learned representation, including class-conditional distance approaches. These scores are intended to flag potentially atypical inputs for analysis; they are not an automatic rejection mechanism.

IG and input interventions are used to study how predictions change when image or text information is altered. These analyses measure model sensitivity under the selected interventions. They do not establish lesion localization, causal reasoning, or clinical correctness.

## Results and limitations

The primary classification results below report mean and standard deviation across the three seeds where available. The CTCH comparison between full multimodal input and image-only input also changes the training procedure, so it does not isolate a causal effect of clinical history alone.

| Dataset / analysis | Result |
|---|---:|
| BTXRD classification, Accuracy / Macro-F1 | 0.8302 / 0.6240 |
| CTCH classification, Accuracy / Macro-F1 | 0.6183 / 0.5727 |
| CTCH image-only Macro-F1 | 0.3813 |
| CTCH full multimodal Macro-F1 | 0.5727 |
| CTCH near-domain OOD AUROC, 669 in-distribution and 18 OOD cases | 0.7598 |
| CTCH OOD FPR at 95% TPR | 0.6296 |
| Updated parameters | about 8.44M (4.13%) |
| Peak training GPU memory reduction vs full fine-tuning | more than 60% |

XBone-Net outperformed ResNet-50 and DenseNet-121 on the reported classification metrics on both datasets, with Holm-adjusted p-values below 0.001. The study did not find a statistically significant difference from full BiomedCLIP fine-tuning. On CTCH, the multimodal configuration achieved a higher Macro-F1 than the image-only configuration, but that comparison includes differences in training stages as well as input modality.

**Key limitations include:**
- **Dataset scope:** CTCH is a single-center private dataset, and the experiments use one fixed partition. External hospital validation is still needed.
- **Text source differs by dataset:** BTXRD uses metadata-derived text, so conclusions about clinical history are limited to CTCH.
- **OOD false positives:** The near-domain OOD FPR at 95% TPR remains high. The reported operating point is not a validated deployment threshold, and the system should not automatically reject cases.
- **Component attribution:** Current ablations do not establish statistically significant independent benefits for every component of the two-stage training and fusion design.
- **Explanation scope:** IG analyses cover one seed and selected interventions; they do not prove that highlighted pixels correspond to lesions or that predictions are clinically valid.
- **Compute trade-offs:** Fewer trainable parameters and lower peak training memory come with longer training and slightly higher inference costs.
- **Clinical status:** XBone-Net is a research framework, not a clinically validated diagnostic system or a replacement for clinician judgment.
